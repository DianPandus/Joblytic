About Finshot
Finshot is a licensed cross-border remittance company. We hold remittance and electronic payment licences in Korea and a funds transfer licence in Japan, and we move real money for real customers every day. Our product and development teams are in Korea, and our development team in Jakarta is growing.



What we are building
Integrations with overseas remittance partners, card networks, and securities firms

B2B cross-border payments across Korea and Japan

AML and compliance systems



About the role
You turn requirements from our Korea team into English specifications that our Jakarta developers can build from. Requirements from Korea are often rough. Your job is to find what they do not say, ask the right questions, and close the gaps before development starts. You use your development background to read the code, understand how the system works today, and write specifications that fit it. This is a hands-on analyst role. You will not manage people, and you will not be assigned feature development.

What you will do
70% Writing English specifications: turn a rough requirement into a specification a developer can implement without further questions (flows, states, API contracts, data changes, error and failure cases, acceptance criteria).

20% Reading and checking code: see how things work now, find what a change will affect, and check that the implementation matches the specification.

10% Supporting developers: answer questions about your specifications and update them when something changes.

Requirements
2+ years of hands-on software development (any backend language)

2+ years in which writing specifications was a main part of your job (FSD, TSD, API specifications, or BRDs that developers actually built from)

Experience working with an external system you did not control; you understand retries, timeouts, idempotency, and reconciliation

Clear written English. Simple, short sentences are what we want

Native or fluent Bahasa Indonesia

Able to work on-site at our Jakarta office every day

When a requirement is vague, your instinct is to ask rather than to guess
